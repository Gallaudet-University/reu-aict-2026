Large language models are increasingly used to "translate" between English and signed languages — sometimes by generating an ASL gloss from English, sometimes by attempting to caption a signed video. These translations look fluent on the surface, but ASL has grammar, depiction, and prosody that English glosses simply cannot represent.

This project examines where these LLM-generated ASL translations fail: which grammatical features they erase, which culturally important elements they miss, and what happens downstream when a deaf reader trusts the output.

**What students do.** Probe several frontier LLMs with ASL-related prompts and tasks, build a taxonomy of failure modes with Deaf collaborators, and write up the findings with an eye toward both the ML community and accessibility policy.

**Skills you might build.** LLM prompting and evaluation, linguistic analysis of ASL, qualitative taxonomy-building, technical writing for mixed audiences.
