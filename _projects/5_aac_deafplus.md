Augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) tools — symbol boards, text-to-speech keyboards, eye-gaze interfaces — are designed almost entirely around speaking, hearing users. They rarely consider users who are deaf and also have other disabilities ("DeafPlus" users), even though many such users rely on AAC daily.

This project runs co-design sessions with DeafPlus AAC users and their communication partners to prototype bilingual ASL/English vocabularies and interaction patterns.

**What students do.** Participate in co-design sessions (this project values listening more than coding), prototype new AAC layouts on tablets, and document design rationale.

**Skills you might build.** Co-design and participatory research methods, accessible prototyping (Figma + lightweight web app), writing about design with and for users with disabilities.
