Most public sign language datasets are recorded in studios under controlled lighting, with signers facing the camera and signing in isolation. Real ASL happens in classrooms, kitchens, dorm rooms, and on Zoom — where lighting is uneven, signing is conversational, and the signer's whole body is moving.

This project asks how well current vision and vision-language models recognize ASL outside the lab, and what changes when we move to "in the wild" video.

**What students do.** Help curate short signed clips, run existing public datasets through off-the-shelf and recent open-source models, and design quantitative error analyses. There's room for systems work (data pipelines, fine-tuning) and qualitative work (categorizing failure modes with Deaf collaborators).

**Skills you might build.** PyTorch, video preprocessing, working with pretrained vision models, designing error taxonomies.
