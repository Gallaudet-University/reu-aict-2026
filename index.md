<section class="section section-alt">
  <div class="container">
    <div class="section-header">
      <h2>About the Program</h2>
      <p class="lead">
        AICT brings deaf, hard-of-hearing, and hearing undergraduates together
        to work alongside faculty on projects that make information and
        communication technology more accessible — and to ask hard questions
        about how today's AI systems shape (and sometimes harm) Deaf
        communication.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards three-up">
      <article class="card">
        <h3>Authentic research</h3>
        <p>
          Each student joins a faculty-led project from day one — contributing
          to a real research question with the goal of producing a poster, paper,
          or working prototype by the end of the summer.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="card">
        <h3>Accessible by design</h3>
        <p>
          The program runs in ASL and English. Lab meetings, workshops, and
          social activities are designed so deaf and hearing students collaborate
          as peers, not as one accommodating the other.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="card">
        <h3>Pathways to graduate study</h3>
        <p>
          Weekly professional development covers reading papers, writing
          abstracts, applying to PhD programs, and presenting research — with
          one-on-one mentoring from faculty and PhD students.
        </p>
      </article>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="container">
    <div class="section-header">
      <h2>Research Themes</h2>
      <p class="lead">
        Projects sit at the intersection of accessibility and modern AI.
        Students pick a project before arrival and work with that team for
        the full ten weeks.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards two-up">
      <article class="card card-accent">
        <h3>Sign language &amp; multimodal AI</h3>
        <p>
          Computer vision for ASL recognition and generation, avatar
          interpretation, fingerspelling, and multimodal models that handle
          signed and spoken language together.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="card card-accent">
        <h3>Captioning &amp; speech access</h3>
        <p>
          Automatic captioning, speaker diarization, sound awareness, and the
          UX of captions on phones, in classrooms, in VR, and in noisy
          real-world settings.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="card card-accent">
        <h3>Accessible HCI</h3>
        <p>
          Designing interfaces with and for deaf, hard-of-hearing, deafblind,
          and DeafDisabled users — including AAC, wearables, and accessible
          XR experiences.
        </p>
      </article>
      <article class="card card-accent">
        <h3>AI fairness for Deaf communities</h3>
        <p>
          How well do today's models work for Deaf users? Auditing speech
          recognition, captioning, and LLMs for bias, error patterns, and
          downstream harms.
        </p>
      </article>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
