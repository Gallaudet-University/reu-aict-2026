## What a week looks like

Most days are spent in your faculty mentor's lab, working on your research project alongside graduate students and other REU peers. Around that core, we schedule shared activities that build skills and community.

- **Mondays** — full-cohort research seminar: each week one student or faculty member presents work-in-progress.
- **Tuesdays / Thursdays** — open lab time. Mentors check in 1:1 with their REU students.
- **Wednesdays** — professional development: reading papers, writing abstracts, IRB and research ethics, applying to graduate school.
- **Fridays** — methods workshops (literature search, study design, statistics, ML tooling, accessible UX evaluation) and social activities.

## Communication access

The program runs in **American Sign Language and English**. Professional ASL/English interpreters are scheduled for all mixed cohort activities, and many faculty mentors sign directly. Lab placements are made so that deaf and hard-of-hearing students have a direct-communication path with their mentor wherever possible.

CART captioning is available on request for any activity. Accessible communication is treated as a design problem the cohort works on together, not just a service we provide.

## Cohort life

Beyond research, the cohort spends time together off campus — exploring DC museums (the Smithsonians are all free), Capitol Hill, Deaf community events, and the National Mall. Past cohorts have also visited research labs at NIH, NIST, Microsoft Research, and Google to see what life as a researcher looks like in industry and government settings.
