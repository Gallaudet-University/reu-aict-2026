## Sign language &amp; multimodal AI

### Continuous ASL recognition in the wild
Build and evaluate models that recognize signed sentences from everyday video — not just isolated signs from clean studio footage. Students will help collect short signed clips, work with existing public datasets, and probe where current vision-language models break.

### Avatar interpretation: what makes a signing avatar legible?
We are running user studies comparing human interpreters, motion-captured signers, and AI-generated signing avatars. Students on this project will help design comprehension and acceptability studies with deaf and hearing viewers.

## Captioning &amp; speech access

### Captioning in noisy classrooms
How well do real-time captioning systems perform when there are overlapping voices, accents, and technical jargon? Students will measure word and concept error rates across off-the-shelf systems and design interfaces that surface uncertainty to the reader.

### Sound awareness for deaf users
Building on prior work on smartwatch- and phone-based sound notifications, this project explores which environmental sounds are most useful to surface, how to display them without overwhelming the user, and how on-device ML can recognize sounds without sending audio to the cloud.

## Accessible HCI

### AAC for DeafPlus users
Augmentative and alternative communication tools rarely consider users who are deaf and also have other disabilities. This project co-designs new AAC interactions with deaf users who use AAC, with a focus on bilingual ASL/English vocabularies.

## AI fairness &amp; the Deaf community

### Auditing speech recognition for Deaf voices
Speech recognition error rates are not the same for everyone. Students on this project will design and run a careful audit comparing ASR performance for hearing speakers vs. deaf speakers with varied speech profiles, and document failure patterns.

### LLMs and ASL gloss
Large language models are increasingly used to generate "captions" or "translations" from ASL. This project examines where those translations fail, what they leave out about grammar and depiction in ASL, and what risks this creates when LLMs sit between deaf and hearing people.
